Part 0 — Dataset, Data Quality & Exploratory Data Analysis¶

Project: Breast Cancer Tumour Diagnosis — Supervised vs Unsupervised Learning Owner of this notebook: K Aditya Raj Kumar Dora (240101230)

Problem statement¶

A Fine Needle Aspirate (FNA) of a breast mass is imaged under a microscope, and 10 properties of the cell nuclei are measured. We want to decide whether the tumour is Malignant (cancerous) or Benign.

  • Program 1 (Supervised): learn the diagnosis from labelled examples → 02_supervised_learning.ipynb
  • Program 2 (Unsupervised): hide the labels and check whether natural groups in the data match the diagnosis → 03_unsupervised_learning.ipynb

This notebook loads and checks the data, explores it, and creates the train/test split that both programs use.

Dataset — Breast Cancer Wisconsin (Diagnostic), UCI ML Repository¶

  • 569 patients, 30 numeric features, 1 label (Malignant / Benign)
  • 10 nucleus measurements: radius, texture, perimeter, area, smoothness, compactness, concavity, concave points, symmetry, fractal dimension
  • Each measurement appears 3 times: mean, standard error, and worst (mean of the 3 largest values) → 10 × 3 = 30 features
  • Source: Wolberg, Street & Mangasarian (1995), University of Wisconsin. Shipped with scikit-learn as load_breast_cancer.

1. Load the data¶

In scikit-learn's copy, target = 0 means malignant. For a medical problem the positive class (1) should be the disease, so we re-encode: target = 1 → Malignant, target = 0 → Benign. Precision and recall then measure how well we catch cancer.

Multicollinearity: radius, perimeter and area are almost perfectly correlated (r ≈ 0.99), which is expected because perimeter ≈ 2πr and area ≈ πr². What this means for the models:

  • tree models are not affected;
  • Logistic Regression needs regularisation (we tune C) so its coefficients stay stable;
  • for clustering, PCA removes the redundancy (see notebook 03).

6. Outliers¶

We use the IQR rule: a value is an outlier if it is below Q1 − 1.5·IQR or above Q3 + 1.5·IQR.

In [12]:
q1, q3 = df[FEATURES].quantile(0.25), df[FEATURES].quantile(0.75)
iqr = q3 - q1
is_out = (df[FEATURES] < q1 - 1.5 * iqr) | (df[FEATURES] > q3 + 1.5 * iqr)
rows_with_outlier = is_out.any(axis=1)
print(f"Rows with at least one outlier: {rows_with_outlier.sum()} / {len(df)}")
print("Malignant share among those rows: "
      f"{df.loc[rows_with_outlier, 'target'].mean():.0%} (vs {df['target'].mean():.0%} overall)")
is_out.sum().sort_values(ascending=False).head(8).to_frame("outlier count")

8. Key findings from EDA¶

  1. Clean data: 569 rows, 30 numeric features, no missing or duplicate values.
  2. Mild class imbalance (37% malignant): use stratification and report recall, F1 and ROC-AUC, not just accuracy.
  3. Very different feature scales: standardise features before KNN, SVM, Logistic Regression and K-Means.
  4. Strong multicollinearity (radius / perimeter / area): use regularisation in supervised models and PCA in unsupervised ones.
  5. Concave points, perimeter, radius and area ("worst" and "mean") are the strongest single indicators of malignancy.
  6. Outliers are real large tumours and are kept.
